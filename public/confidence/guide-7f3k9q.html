12 Weeks to Confident Approaching

A week-by-week training plan that takes you from avoiding eye contact to walking up to a group of girls in broad daylight, being playful, and inviting one of them out. No scripts, no weird tactics. Reps, structure, and accountability.

Who I am and why this exists

My name is Will. A few years ago I was the guy this guide is written for. I could talk to anyone at work, I was fine around friends, but the idea of walking up to a girl I had never met, in daylight, sober, with people around, made my chest tighten. I would see someone, think about it for thirty seconds, build the whole conversation in my head, and then keep walking. Every single time.

What finally changed things for me was not a magic line or a personality transplant. It was realizing that approaching is a physical skill, like a golf swing or a deadlift. Nobody walks into a gym and pulls 400 pounds on day one, and nobody should expect to charm a group of strangers on day one either. Skills are built with progressive reps. Small, almost embarrassingly easy at first, then a little heavier each week.

So that is what I did. I gave myself tiny assignments. Week one was literally just making eye contact with strangers and counting how many. It felt like nothing. But nothing stacked on nothing turned into something, and about three months in I caught myself joking around with three girls outside a coffee shop and asking one of them out without a single rehearsal in my head. The nervous guy was still in there somewhere, but he was not driving anymore.

Afterward I went back through my notes and asked: what actually moved the needle? I cut everything that did not, kept the handful of steps that did, and put them in order. That is this guide. It is my own path, cleaned up and distilled into a simple playbook you can run in 12 weeks. I have since run it with private clients, and the pattern holds: the guys who do the reps in order get there. The guys who skip ahead stall.

In my own story and in working with men like yourself, I have found that your most attractive self is the most confident version of you: the one who's comfortable enough to stop performing and just be himself. This guide will slowly strip away the fear so that guy can show up in more and more situations that used to scare you.

The one rule: trust the process

Do every week in order, even the ones that feel too easy for you. Maybe you already make eye contact fine. Maybe complimenting a guy's watch sounds beneath you. Do it anyway. The early weeks are not there to teach you a trick, they are there to build a reflex: see, decide, act, hundreds of times, until hesitation loses its grip. Every guy I have seen skip a "too easy" week paid for it two levels up. Run the weeks in order, hit the rep counts, and let the pyramid do its job.

How to use this guide

  • Everything happens in full daylight. Streets, coffee shops, grocery stores, parks, bookstores, the gym lobby. No bars, no clubs, no alcohol. Daylight is harder at first and that is exactly why it works: confidence built sober at 2pm is real everywhere.
  • Each week has a mission, rep counts, and a scorecard. Check the boxes as you go. In week 8 you will love flipping back to week 1 and seeing how far you have come.
  • Do the End of Week Reflection once each week is finished. Not before. Complete the reps, fill in the scorecard, then give it two minutes of honest writing. This is where the lessons actually stick.
  • Every outing, at every level, begins with eye contact. After week 1 you stop counting it, but you never stop leading with it: eyes up, chin level, present, from the first minute. Each week's warm-up ladder starts there and climbs through the levels you have already built before you touch the new one. And the lower levels stay on the menu for the whole outing: between reps, while you look for your next one, or any time the new level feels heavy, drop down a level or two and stack easy wins. That still counts as training.
  • A rep only counts if it is a stranger. Not your barista you already know, not a coworker. New people only.
  • If you miss a week, repeat it. Do not skip forward to catch up. The order matters more than the calendar.
  • Be a gentleman, always. Every level of this pyramid includes reading the other person and gracefully leaving people alone who want to be left alone. That is not a side note, it is a core skill you will train explicitly. The goal is to leave everyone you talk to a little better off than you found them.

Before you start: your baseline

Answer these honestly. You will read them again in week 12 and laugh.

When was the last time you started a conversation with a girl you had never met, in person, during the day? What happened?
On a scale of 1 to 10, how much fear do you feel at the idea of walking up to a girl on the street right now? What does that fear physically feel like?
List three daylight spots near you with steady foot traffic (a main street, a shopping district, a park, a big grocery store). These are your training grounds for the next 12 weeks.

The Playbook On One Page

The Approach Pyramid

Every week of this guide trains one layer of this pyramid. Each layer rests on the one below it. You cannot hold a playful conversation if eye contact still spikes your heart rate, and you cannot invite a girl out if you cannot yet tell whether she is enjoying the conversation. Build from the bottom. By week 12 the whole pyramid is yours.

Level 7 · Week 12 The Full Approach Group, playful, daylight, invitation
Level 6 · Week 11 Groups One person at a time
Level 5 · Week 10 The Invitation Say what you want, hear any answer
Level 4 · Weeks 8-9 Conversations & Checking In Say hi, check in, continue or exit
Level 3 · Weeks 6-7 Direct Compliments "I saw you and had to say something"
Level 2 · Weeks 3-5 Compliments Guys first, then hair, shoes, clothes
Level 1 · Weeks 1-2 Presence & Eye Contact Get out, look up, count everything
Why a pyramid and not a list?

A list says "here are ten things to try." A pyramid says "you are not allowed to the next level until you have solid footing on the current step." When you finally give a girl a direct compliment in week 6, you will have already made hundreds of pieces of eye contact and given dozens of low-stakes compliments. Your body will know, from evidence, that nothing bad happens. That is what confidence actually is: stacked evidence.

The shape also tells you the volume. The wide levels at the bottom are the things you will do the most, by far: eye contact and compliments happen dozens of times a week for the rest of your life. The higher you climb, the less often each rep occurs; you might give hundreds of compliments in the time it takes to give ten invitations, and that ratio is exactly right. And you never retire a level. Reaching conversations does not mean you stop giving compliments; reaching invitations does not mean you stop holding eye contact. Every rep at a higher level sits on live reps from all the levels beneath it, every single day.

WEEK 1Level 1 · Presence & Eye Contact

Get Out and Look Up

Mission: leave the house 5 days this week, and on each outing make distinct eye contact with 10 strangers. Count every single one.

What "distinct" means

Not a dart-away glance. You look at their eyes, they register that you are looking, and you hold it for about one full second before either of you moves on. Relaxed face, chin level, shoulders back. You are not staring anyone down and you are not smiling yet.

Anyone counts this week: men, women, old, young. This is not about girls yet.

Why start with eye contact?

Because it is the bedrock. Most nervous guys have spent years actively avoiding eye contact, and that avoidance quietly trains the belief that being noticed is dangerous. Every level of this pyramid involves being seen, so we retrain your default posture first: from "invisible" to "present." Fix the bedrock and everything above it gets easier.

The reps

  • 5 outings, minimum 20 minutes each, in one of your three training grounds.
  • 10 distinct eye contacts per outing. Count them on your fingers or in your phone.
  • Phone stays in your pocket while you walk. Headphones off. You cannot see eyes you are not looking for.
Feels too easy?

Perfect. It is supposed to. Do it anyway and hit the full count. Guys who "already make eye contact fine" almost always discover on day one that they hold it for a tenth of a second and bail. Get the real number, not the imagined one.

WEEK 2Level 1 · Presence & Eye Contact

Eye Contact Plus a Smile

Mission: 5 outings. 10 eye contacts per outing as before, but now you add a small relaxed smile or a nod, and to at least 3 people per outing you say a word: "morning," "hey," "how's it going."

The upgrade

This week you add warmth. The smile is small, the kind you would give a neighbor. The nod is one dip of the chin. The greeting is thrown as you pass, no stopping, no waiting for a reply. If they answer, great. If they do not, you have already moved on and lost nothing.

Start including women in your reps naturally, roughly half and half.

Why track who smiles back?

Because your brain has been running on a prediction that strangers are hostile, and predictions only change with data. When you smile first, a surprising number of people smile back. Counting them turns a vague "that went okay" into hard evidence your nervous system cannot argue with.

The reps

  • 5 outings, 10 eye contacts with a smile or nod each.
  • 3 verbal greetings per outing, thrown in passing.
  • Track how many people smile back or reply.

WEEK 3Level 2 · Compliments, Tier 1

Compliment Guys First

Mission: 5 outings. On each one, give 3 genuine compliments to other guys. Nice watch, cool shoes, great jacket. Say it, mean it, keep moving.

Warm-up · every outingStart every outing the way you started week 1: eyes up. Spend your first five minutes on deliberate eye contact, no counting required anymore, just presence, plus a couple of smiles and one thrown greeting. Then start the compliments.
Why compliment guys first?

Because there are zero stakes. No attraction, no agenda, no fear of "hitting on" anyone. Which means all of your attention goes to the actual skill: notice something real, say it with a normal voice, and be fine with whatever comes back. Everything above this level is built on that.

There is a bonus, too. Guys light up when another guy compliments their watch. You are about to spend a week making strangers' days better, and you will start to feel like the kind of man who does that. That identity shift matters more than it sounds.

How to do it

  • It must be true. Only say it if you actually noticed it and actually like it.
  • Say it in passing or at a natural pause. Two to six words is plenty.
  • Deliver it like a statement, not a question. Then keep walking or return to what you were doing. If he starts a chat, ride it for a minute, then "have a good one."
"Hey man, I like your shoes." · "Cool shoes dude." · "Nice watch man." · "Looking jacked, my man."
The resistance tool starts now

From this week to the end of the guide, this is how you handle hesitation. The moment you feel yourself tighten up before a rep, do two things: breathe deeper, and name it in your head: "I am feeling resistance." That is it. Do not force yourself forward while you are half in, half out; a forced rep comes out strained and teaches you nothing. Naming the resistance separates you from it, and the deeper breath settles your body enough to move.

To be clear about what "settled" means: the goal is not to wait until the fear disappears. It will not. Take the breath, let the initial spike pass, and then act while you are still nervous. You are training yourself to be you with fear present, not waiting for perfect confidence before you move.

WEEK 4Level 2 · Compliments, Tier 2

Style Compliments to Girls

Mission: 5 outings. On each one, stop 2 girls and compliment something they chose: hair, shoes, jacket, bag, nails. The exact shape: "Hey, I really like your x. Have a great day." Then you leave. You are not starting conversations yet.

Warm-up · every outingEyes up first: a few minutes of deliberate eye contact and smiles as you walk in. Then 1 compliment to a guy to get your voice warm. Then start the reps.

The tier 2 rules

This is the same skill as last week aimed at a new target, and the aim matters. You compliment choices, not bodies. Her hair color, her sneakers, her jacket, her tattoo. Things she picked out and hoped people would notice. A compliment on a choice says "you have good taste" and lands as a gift. Save anything about her looks for week 6; you have not earned that level yet.

Why stop her, just to walk away?

Because the stop and the exit are each doing a job. This is not a drive-by: you stop her, step into her line of sight, plant your feet, and put yourself squarely in front of her attention for a moment. That is half the training. Every time you do it and the world does not end, you collect another piece of evidence that being noticed is not dangerous. Most nervous guys have never tested that; you are about to test it ten times.

The other half is the ending. Closing with "Have a great day" is built into the line on purpose: it releases all the pressure of what comes next. You are not fishing for a conversation, so there is no conversation to perform. You stand in front of her, give the gift, wish her well, and go. She gets a nice moment, and your nervous system learns that being seen costs nothing.

How to do it

  • On the sidewalk, in a store aisle, in a line. Anywhere natural, always daylight.
  • Approach from the front or side, a soft "excuse me" or "hey" to stop her, eye contact, small smile.
  • Say the line: "Hey, I really like your x. Have a great day." Then actually leave. The whole thing takes a few seconds.
  • She will almost always smile and say "thank you!" That is a perfect rep. You do not need her to do anything more than that.
  • If she is wearing headphones, rushing, or clearly occupied, pick someone else. Reading who is open to a moment is part of the rep.
"Hey, I really like your hair. Have a great day." · "Excuse me, I really like those shoes. Have a good one." · "Hey, that jacket is awesome. Have a great day."
Already comfortable complimenting girls?

Then this is your easiest week all program, and you still do all 10 reps. You are not here to prove you can do it once, you are here to make it so automatic that in week 12, with a group in front of you, it costs zero willpower.

WEEK 5Level 2 · Compliments, Tier 2 Plus

Compliment, Then Stay a Beat

Mission: 5 outings, 2 stop-and-compliment reps each, same as last week, but now you drop the built-in "have a great day" ending. Stay for her response and add one follow-up line before you leave.

Warm-up · every outingEyes up first, a couple of smiles, then 1 compliment to a guy. Climb the ladder you built, then begin the reps.

The upgrade

Last week you closed the door yourself before she could even answer. This week you leave it open for five extra seconds. Give the compliment, hold relaxed eye contact, let her say thanks, then add one light follow-up. Then, and this is still the rule, you end it and leave first.

Why leave first while it is going well?

Two reasons. You are training your body to be comfortable inside the small window where a conversation could start, without any pressure to start one. And leaving first a few times is useful practice: it teaches you that you don't need to cling to an interaction just because it's going well.

How to do it

  • Best done when you are both stationary: in a line, a store, a coffee shop.
  • Compliment, pause, receive her response with a smile.
  • One follow-up: a quick question or an honest observation, whatever is actually on your mind.
  • Then exit warmly: "Anyway, have a great one."
"Love the jacket. ... Where'd you find it?" · "Cool tattoo. ... Is there a story behind it?"

WEEK 6Level 3 · Direct Compliments

Say What You Actually Mean

Mission: 5 outings. On each one, give 1 direct compliment: tell a girl you think she looks great, directly and honestly, then wish her a good day and go. Keep doing 3 style compliments per outing as a warm-up.

Warm-up · every outingYour main goal today is a single rep, so load up on reinforcement first. Eyes up from the first minute, a handful of smiles and thrown greetings, 2 or 3 compliments to guys, then your 3 style-compliment stop-and-releases from the mission. That is ten easy wins before the big one, and by the time you give the direct compliment you are already the guy who has been talking to strangers all afternoon.

The line you are crossing

Why say it so directly?

"Nice shoes" could come from anyone. "I think you look great" can only come from a man being honest about why he walked over. This is the week you stop hiding your intent, and it is the biggest single jump in the program. That is why everything below it exists: four weeks of evidence that strangers respond well to you. You are ready.

The structure is simple and honest: acknowledge the interruption, give the compliment, release her. No follow-up question this week. You deliver it, she reacts, you say "have a great day," and you walk. You are proving to yourself that you can tell a woman you find her attractive without hiding it, apologizing for it, or needing anything to happen afterward.

How to do it

  • Approach from the front or side, never from behind. Comfortable distance, hands visible and relaxed.
  • Speak slower and a touch louder than feels natural. Nerves make you fast and quiet.
  • Smile with it. This is a compliment, not a confession.
  • Whatever her reaction: warm smile, surprised laugh, awkward thanks, you respond the same way: "Have a great day," and go. Every reaction is a successful rep this week.
  • Same reading rules as always: headphones, hurry, or closed-off body language means pick a different moment with someone else.
  • The compliment has to be true, which means you actually have to find her attractive. Some outings, nobody stops you in your tracks. Do not force it on someone you are not drawn to; spend that outing stacking the old stuff instead: eye contact, greetings, guy compliments, style compliments. The outing still counts as training, and you pick up the direct-compliment rep on a day when someone genuinely catches your eye.
"Excuse me. I saw you and I had to say: you're really pretty. Have a great day." · "Hey, I saw you from over there and had to come tell you: you look great today. Have a good one."
This week will spike your nerves. Good.

The first direct compliment of your life could be genuinely hard. That is normal. When you notice the resistance, breathe a little deeper and say in your head, "I am feeling resistance." Then go, nerves and all. After a few reps you will probably notice how much less dramatic this feels than you expected.

WEEK 7Level 3 · Direct Compliments + The Check-In

Direct Compliments, Eyes Open

Mission: 5 outings, 1 direct compliment each, but now, right after each one, you check in with yourself and log how it felt: green, yellow, or red. Comfortable? Confident? Nervous? Forced? Knowing your own state in the moment is the skill.

Warm-up · every outingSame heavy ladder as week 6: eyes up, smiles and greetings freely, 2 or 3 compliments to guys, 1 or 2 style-compliment stop-and-releases. One direct-compliment rep a day means plenty of room to keep every lower level sharp; use it.

The check-in

As your compliment lands, take one breath and notice how you feel standing there: your breathing, your voice, your shoulders, your gut. Then exit warmly as always, and log the color.

Why grade yourself instead of her?

Because you are the half of the moment you control, and your own state tells you almost everything. When you are settled, you talk normally, you notice her naturally, and the moment feels clean on both sides. When you are forcing it, everything downstream goes wrong no matter what you say. From here to the top of the pyramid everything runs on one loop, act, check in, decide, and this week you train the check-in with nothing riding on it. Next week you start acting on it.

Green · Comfortable
  • Breathing easy, normal talking speed
  • You meant every word
  • You enjoyed the moment instead of surviving it
  • You could feel how it was landing with her
  • You would happily do it again right now
Yellow · Nervous
  • Heart pounding, words rushed
  • Half in, half out as you said it
  • Already walking away before you finished
  • Mostly relieved that it is over
Red · Forced
  • You did not really mean it
  • You pushed through when it did not feel right
  • You stayed after she showed she was done
  • It felt needy or creepy in your own gut
Her side is simple

Your colors grade you, but her side is never up for debate: any sign she is not interested or not comfortable, and the interaction is over, instantly and warmly. "Have a great day" and gone, zero persistence, zero commentary. Most of those moments are about her day, not about you, and exiting gracefully costs you nothing. A yellow or red in your log is not a verdict on you either; it is information. Nerves fade with reps, and a forced rep just means you climbed too fast that day. Drop down a level, get comfortable, climb again.

WEEK 8Level 4 · Conversations & Checking In

Start the Conversation

Mission: 4 outings. On each one, walk up to 2 girls and start with a direct compliment (or a plain hello) and, on any green or warm yellow, stay and hold a 2 to 5 minute conversation. On a red, exit warmly as always. Target: at least 3 real conversations this week.

Warm-up · every outingLadder up before the conversations: eye contact from the moment you arrive, 1 compliment to a guy, 1 style-compliment stop-and-release. Five minutes of that and you reach your first walk-up already warm.

The loop goes live

Everything you have built now clicks together. You start the way you have started for weeks: a compliment, or the honest reason you came over: "Hey, I saw you and wanted to come say hi. I'm [name]." Then you check in, exactly like week 7. Feeling green and she is enjoying it: you stay. Forced, or she is not interested: "have a great day," gone. The decision takes one breath and you have already practiced both branches dozens of times.

Holding the conversation

  • Introduce yourself early. Names turn a moment into a conversation. Offer yours first.
  • Talk like yourself. No material, no lines. Say what you actually think, ask what you are actually curious about, and react honestly to what she says. The conversation does not need to be impressive, it needs to be real.
  • Keep checking in as you go. The week 7 check-in does not stop after the compliment. How do you feel, minute to minute, and is she adding to the conversation or just answering? When it stops feeling good on either side, wrap it warmly.
  • Silence is fine. A two-second pause is just a pause. Do not sprint to fill it.
  • End it yourself while it is still good. "Hey, I've got to run. This was lovely." Ending it yourself while it is still fun also sets up everything about week 10.
"Excuse me. I saw you over there and thought you looked great, and I'd have kicked myself for not saying hi. I'm [name]." After that, no script. It is just a conversation.
Redefine the win

A "failed" conversation does not exist this week. A red at the compliment is a successful read. A chat that fizzles after ninety seconds is a successful exit. Log everything.

WEEK 9Level 4 · Conversations & Checking In

Longer, Looser Conversations

Mission: 4 outings, 2 walk-ups each, aiming for 4+ conversations of 5 minutes or more. Same skill as last week, more time in it, less effort spent performing.

Warm-up · every outingSame ladder: eyes up, a guy compliment, a style compliment, then go start your conversations.

From careful to comfortable

Week 8 conversations were probably a little careful, and that is fine. This week the goal is simply more time in the water. Longer conversations, held a little more loosely, until talking to a girl you just met starts to feel like talking to anyone else you get along with.

Why no lines or techniques?

You do not need them, and they would make you worse. You already know how to have a good conversation; you have them all the time with people you are comfortable around. Comfort is the only missing piece, and comfort comes from the reps you are already doing, not from material. Say what you actually think, ask what you are actually curious about, laugh when something is actually funny. Her favorite version of this conversation is the one where you are not performing.

How to do it

  • Let it run longer than feels natural. The five-minute mark is where most guys bail out of habit, not because the conversation died. Stay past the itch to escape.
  • If you fumble, keep going. A stumble only becomes awkward if you treat it like a disaster. Everyone fumbles; comfortable people just continue.
  • Keep checking in. The week 7 check-in runs the whole conversation. Feeling green and she is in it: keep going. Starting to force it: wrap it warmly.
  • Still end it yourself while it is good. Leaving on a high note stays the rule, and next week it becomes the moment you give the invitation.

WEEK 10Level 5 · The Invitation

Make the Invitation

Mission: 4 outings, 2 walk-ups each. In every conversation that stays green, give an invitation. Target: 3 invitations given this week, regardless of the answers you get.

Warm-up · every outingLadder up as always: eyes up on arrival, a compliment or two on the way in. Invitations land best when you are already loose.

Don't seek validation

This week, when a conversation is going well, you tell her you would like to see her again and suggest something concrete: "I would love to see you again. Coffee this weekend?"

Why say it like this?

Make no mistake: you are asking her out, and that is fine. Being genuinely confident includes being comfortable making yourself vulnerable to an answer. The wording matters for a different reason. "Can I get your number?" quietly makes the moment about whether you win something from her, and that is where the neediness creeps in. "I would love to see you again, coffee this weekend?" keeps the moment about what you actually want, said plainly, with room for her to want it too.

So do not make this about getting validation or collecting a number. You are expressing what you want and letting her answer be whatever it is. That is the whole skill, and it is why a no costs you nothing this week.

How to do it

  • Invite while it is still fun, at the natural peak, not after the conversation has wilted. Often that is minute four, not minute twenty.
  • The shape: honest statement, concrete idea, open door. "This has been the best part of my day. I'd love to see you again. There's a great coffee spot on 5th; we should go some morning."
  • Then give her a second to answer. Say it, smile, and wait. Do not backpedal, do not add "no pressure haha," do not fill the silence.
  • If she is in: she will say yes or reach for the logistics herself. "Perfect. What's your number? I'll text you and we'll set it up." Getting the number is now an afterthought of the plan, not the goal.
  • A soft maybe: "maybe," "I'm pretty busy" with warmth. Stay easy: "All good. Take my number and use it if you feel like it." Leave the door open and exit first, warmly.
  • A no: any form of it. "All good, this was still the best chat I've had today. Have a great one." Mean it. A graceful no costs you nothing; you will be giving another invitation tomorrow.

WEEK 11Level 6 · Groups

Approaching Groups

Mission: 4 outings. First half of the week: give your week 4 stop-and-release compliment to one girl who is with her friends (a small, deliberate step back down the pyramid). Second half: same walk-up, but stay and hold a conversation with her. Target: 4 compliments to a girl in a group, then 2 group conversations.

Warm-up · every outingEyes up and a couple of solo compliments before anything else, every outing. On days 3 and 4, a stop-and-release compliment to a girl in a group is itself the warm-up for staying in one.

The truth about groups

Why are groups the last step?

Because girls usually are not out alone. They shop together, get coffee together, walk together. Being able to talk to a girl who is with her friends simply gives you more chances to meet the right woman for you. That is all this level is: the same skills you already have, usable in more of real life.

And nothing about this week replaces what you already do. When you see a girl on her own, take that rep too, this week and every week after. One-on-one interactions stay on the menu for good; groups are an addition, not a replacement.

Groups look intimidating, and here is the thing that shrinks them: you are never talking to a group. You are only ever talking to one person at a time. Trying to address three people at once is what overwhelms guys; it splits your attention and turns you into an entertainer working a crowd. So do not do it. Pick your girl, tell her why you came over, and talk to her. When a friend chimes in, talk to that friend, directly, then come back. Each moment is just one conversation with one person. Nothing rehearsed, nothing repeated; the same honesty you have been practicing for weeks, aimed at one face at a time.

Her friends are not an obstacle. They are just other people standing there. Do not ignore them, do not try to win them over, and do not perform for the group. If someone joins the conversation, talk to her normally too.

Step one: compliment one girl in a group (days 1 and 2)

You are never complimenting the group. Complimenting a group of girls is hard; complimenting one girl who happens to be standing with her friends is easy, because it is exactly your week 4 skill. Same stop, same honesty, same "have a great day," same walking away. The only new thing is that her friends are watching, and after a rep or two you will notice that changes nothing. You are teaching your body that an audience is fine.

To one girl, friends right there: "Hey, I really like your jacket. Have a great day." · "Excuse me, those shoes are great. Have a good one."

Step two: walk up to the group, talk to the one (days 3 and 4)

  • Go straight to your girl. You do not have to address the group, win the room, or say anything to anyone else first. That is exactly the part that scares guys, and it is not required. Walk up, look at her, and give her the compliment or the honest reason you came over, the same as any solo approach.
  • Every moment is one-on-one. Whether you are talking to your girl or to one of her friends, you are only ever talking to one person at a time. Everything you know from weeks 8 and 9 applies unchanged.
  • Do not ignore her friends. Acknowledge them, and when you do, talk to them individually: turn to one friend, say the thing, then back to your girl. One person, one moment. Do not let yourself get overwhelmed trying to hold the whole group at once.
  • When a friend jumps in, talk to her too. Directly, one-on-one, the same way you talk to anyone. Then come back to your girl. You are never managing "the group"; you are just having a series of one-person conversations while standing in front of a few people.
  • Check in as always. How does it feel, and is she enjoying it? If the group is mid-goodbye or clearly mid-serious-talk, pick another moment. If she is not interested, warm exit for everyone: "You all have a great day."
Why the step back down is not a step back

Dropping from invitations back to style compliments might feel like remedial work. It is not; it is how you climb with a new variable. You change one thing at a time: same skill, new headcount, then same headcount, higher skill. That is exactly how you got here, and it is why days 3 and 4 will feel strangely doable.

WEEK 12Level 7 · The Full Approach

The Whole Pyramid at Once

Mission: broad daylight. Walk up to a group of girls, go straight to the one, hold a fun conversation, keep checking in, and if it is green, give her the invitation. Do the full approach at least twice this week.

Warm-up · every outingThe same ladder that got you here: eyes up from the first minute, smiles and greetings, a couple of compliments to guys, a style-compliment stop-and-release or two. Get loose, then find your group.

Graduation day

The mission is a group because that is where the girls are: most of the women you will want to meet will be out with friends, which is exactly why this is the graduation skill. And when one is on her own, everything from weeks 8 through 10 is right there; keep taking those one-on-one reps whenever the moment shows up, this week and forever.

Read that mission again, then flip back to your week 0 baseline. Twelve weeks ago that paragraph described someone else's life. Now look at what it actually is: eye contact you mastered in week 1, dozens of honest compliments behind you, checking in with yourself from week 7, conversations from weeks 8 and 9, an invitation from week 10, and the group skill from week 11. There is not one new skill on graduation day. There is only the pyramid, and you own every level of it.

The run-sheet

  • Pick your spot and ladder up like every other week: eyes up from the moment you arrive, a few compliments on the way in. If resistance shows up, you know the tool: breathe a little deeper, say "I am feeling resistance" in your head, and go when you are settled.
  • Walk up to your girl and tell her the truth about why you came over: the compliment, or the reason. Her friends do not need a separate hello.
  • Have fun with it. Be yourself, laugh easily, and when her friends jump in, talk to them the same way: directly, one person at a time.
  • Keep checking in. Green: keep going. Nervous: breathe deeper, name it, settle. Forced, or she is not interested: warm exit for the whole group, head high; that is a fully successful rep too.
  • On green, at the peak, make the invitation: "This was the best part of my day. I would love to see you again." Give her a second to answer, and land it gracefully either way.
One more time: the rep is the win

Whether she says yes, maybe, or no, you did the thing this guide exists to teach: walked up to a group of strangers in daylight, focused on the one you wanted, and honestly offered to see her again. Keep doing that and let the outcomes be what they are.

These skills fade if you stop using them, so keep a light maintenance dose: a few compliments a week, a conversation or two, an invitation whenever a conversation earns one. If life pulls you away for a month and the rust creeps in, do not start over; just drop two levels down the pyramid for a week and climb back up. You built it once with no map.
